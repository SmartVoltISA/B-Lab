# EXP-0011 — Compression tool comparison

## Purpose

Do not assume that the local B-Lab compressor is the best general-purpose compressor. Compare it against established lossless codecs and against structural compression before choosing a default.

## Candidate families

### A. Structural / semantic compression
- B-Lab compact representation: `initial + ordered targets`.
- Goal: remove redundancy by exploiting known binary transition structure before a generic codec.

### B. General-purpose codecs
- Zstandard (zstd): balanced speed/ratio; strong general-purpose candidate.
- Brotli: often strong ratio on structured text; slower at high quality.
- LZ4: speed-first; weaker ratio but very fast.
- gzip/DEFLATE: compatibility-first baseline.
- XZ/LZMA2: ratio-first archival candidate; slower.

## Comparison dimensions

1. compressed size;
2. compression time;
3. decompression time;
4. memory/CPU cost;
5. round-trip exactness;
6. behavior on tiny payloads;
7. behavior on long repetitive binary histories;
8. behavior on irregular/random histories;
9. ability to stream;
10. implementation complexity.

## Important rule

The benchmark must compare **the same logical data**. For B-Lab structural compression, compare both:

- raw sequence → generic codec;
- compact structural representation → generic codec.

This separates gains from representation from gains supplied by the codec itself.

## Current external evidence

Recent public benchmarks consistently show a trade-off rather than one universal winner: Zstd is a strong general-purpose balance, LZ4 favors speed, Brotli often favors ratio on text, and XZ/LZMA2 can favor maximum archival ratio at higher CPU cost. A 2026 empirical study also reports a message-size break-even point where compression overhead can make small payloads larger; therefore B-Lab must include a no-compression path.

## Preliminary decision

Do not select a single codec yet. Build a benchmark tool and choose by workload. The B-Lab structural representation remains a separate candidate because it changes the information representation itself rather than merely applying another codec.
